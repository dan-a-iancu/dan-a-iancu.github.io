abstract: "Smallholder households growing commodities such as cocoa often depend on their children’s labor to secure basic subsistence. We model their borrowing, saving, consumption, and child-labor decisions and uncover sharp trade-offs: better credit access can reduce or increase child labor depending on why a household borrows; better savings access consistently reduces child labor but can lower consumption; and price premiums reduce child labor only when sufficiently large, otherwise they may increase it. Effective interventions must therefore be tailored to household circumstances."
